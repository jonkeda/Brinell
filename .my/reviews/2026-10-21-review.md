# Brinell review, 2026-10-21: Core's architecture, Html and MAUI speed and flakiness

A read of `move` at a7d989c7 (nothing was run), asked for by Tomorrow's SDLC review of the same
day (`.sdlc/reviews/2026-10-21-src-tests-sdlc.md` in the parent repository). Three parts, as the
owner asked: Brinell.Core's architecture, then performance and flakiness on Html, then on MAUI's
Android and iOS drivers. Line numbers are of the files at that commit.

## Outcome

The call model is sound and is the right place to fix flakiness: one poll per public call, one
budget, a confirmation phase with its own budget, a stale element as a signal, a configuration
error ending the call at once. The weak points sit around it. Core carries two generations side by
side (2,035 of its 7,499 lines are the pre-call-model interfaces that only WPF, WinForms and
Stride still use), the three modern stacks each keep their own copy of the control, container and
collection bases (3,420 lines in MAUI, 932 in Html, 1,157 in native Android), thirteen wait loops
with budgets of their own still run below the poll, and the poll's pause spins instead of
sleeping. On Html the cost per call is round trips (a page probe is three Playwright calls before
the element is looked up) and no failure gets a screenshot; on Android it is the same round trips
at a hundred times the price, plus a driver session that dies with the UiAutomator2 server.

The four things to do first: make the pause a sleep and give controls a predicate wait on an
attribute (Tomorrow's seven sleeps become one call each); read a Blazor page's readiness in one
trip and let Playwright's own actionability wait replace Brinell's visibility loop; give Html a
screenshot on failure; and do the move-down of the `Run*` bases that AD-010 has promised since
2026-09-26, so a fix lands in one file instead of three.

## Brinell.Core: what is clean

`Calls/` is small and does what the contract says: `Poller.cs` (71 lines) is the one retry loop,
`ControlCall.cs` (107) the one log pair and budget, `Confirmer.cs` (64) the second phase with its
own `AttemptContext`, `ScopeGate.cs` (48) the readiness step, and `AttemptContext.Current` an
`AsyncLocal` so a driver call below gets what is left of the budget without a parameter.
`ScopeReadiness` names who was not ready and why, so a failure message says "the page is busy"
rather than "not found". Recording costs a null check when nothing records
(`Recording/Interactions.cs:118`), recorders are found by an assembly attribute, and the narrator's
`BddSceneRecording` is just an `IBddStepPacer`. The MCP server is thin: 37 tools in one list and
one name switch over the language server (`BddMcpServer.cs:35-72`), no model call.

## Brinell.Core: what is tangled

**Two generations in one project.** `Interfaces/` (2,035 lines) is the old shape: `IDriver<T>`
with a wait in every lookup (`IDriver.cs:22`, `FindElement(locator, timeoutMs = 5000)`),
`IElement<T>` with its own `ScrollIntoView(timeoutMs = 5000)`, `IPageObject` with
`PageReadinessSnapshot` and `BusySignalPolicy`. `Utilities/WaitHelper.cs` (219 lines) holds five
wait loops that swallow every exception (lines 43, 82, 163, 199). Html, MAUI and native Android
derive from none of it (their interface files say so at `IHtmlElement.cs:7`, `IMauiTestContext.cs:6`,
`INativeAndroidElement.cs:3`); WPF (5 files), WinForms (3) and Stride (3) do. So Core's public
surface describes a model its contract forbids, and a reader of `IDriver.cs` learns the wrong thing.

**Three copies of the bases.** The `Run*` family and `ActOnce` are the same code three times:
Html `Controls/ControlBase.tpl.cs` (654 lines; `ActOnce` at 262-279), MAUI `Controls/Base/ViewBase.tpl.cs`
(806) and `Containers/ContainerObjectBase.cs` (874; `ActOnceOnRoot` at 731-748), native Android
`Controls/Base/ViewBase.tpl.cs` (669). Containers and collections likewise: Html 142 and 136 lines,
MAUI 874 and 1,071, native Android 488 in all. AD-010 makes this deliberate until the move-down;
the move-down has been "the next step" since 2026-09-26 and the copies have started to differ
(Html's `RunGetWithOptionalElement` polls, MAUI's runs once through `Run`).

**The pause spins.** `WaitHelper.Pause` is `SpinWait.SpinUntil(static () => false, ms)`
(`WaitHelper.cs:22`), which yields and `Sleep(0)`s until the time is up rather than blocking; the
comment above it names avoiding `Thread.Sleep` as the aim. It is the pause of `Poller.cs:63`,
`Confirmer.cs:61` and the three `ActOnce` loops, so every one of Tomorrow's ten parallel collections
burns a core between attempts on the machine that also runs the app under test and SQL Server.

**Thirteen waits below the poll.** R2 says nothing below a call loops or sleeps; these do, each with
a budget the caller cannot shorten: `UatControlAssertions.cs:90`, `Maui/Testing/NavigationRecovery.cs:128`,
`AppiumMauiElement.cs:725`, `:823` and `:1404`, `AppiumMauiElement.Pickers.cs:259`,
`AppiumMauiElement.Wheels.cs:220`, `AppiumMauiDriver.FindChrome` and `FindAllChrome` on Selenium's
`WebDriverWait` (`AppiumMauiDriver.cs:84-121`), `FlaUIMauiDriver.cs:581` and `:642`,
`FlaUIMauiElement.cs:685`, `:1146`, `:1173`, `:1271` and `:1499`, `Bridge/BridgeVerbRunner.cs:345`.
(`WebAppHost.cs:198` and `BddBreakpoints.cs:337` are the allowed kind: no UI yet, a file write.)

**State in statics.** `Interactions._log` is one log for the process (`Recording/Interactions.cs:78`):
two collections recording at once would write each other's taps into their scenes.
`UatScenarioTestBase._currentFile` is static per closed type (`UatScenarioTestBase.cs:166`), so two
collections alternating files call `BeforeFile` again on every switch. `ScreenshotTestAttribute`
keeps five `AsyncLocal`s that a fixture sets from its constructor (`MauiTestFixtureBase.cs:147`),
which only reach the tests that share that flow. `PhysicalInput` and `MauiTestFixtureBase._instanceCount`
are harmless but of the same kind.

**Platform knowledge above the driver.** Eight MAUI control and container files branch on
`MauiPlatform` (ten branches) and five carry `XCUIElementType` or `android.widget` literals:
`IndicatorView.tpl.cs:112-148`, `ScrollView.tpl.cs`, `ContentDialog.tpl.cs`, `Expander.tpl.cs`,
`RatingView.tpl.cs`, the two container bases and `ViewBase.tpl.cs`. `ShellChrome` shows the right
shape: the driver answers with locators (`AppiumMauiDriver.cs:155-179`) and the control stays
platform-free.

## Html: how a call is spent

On a Blazor page a `Click` makes about six Playwright round trips per attempt before Playwright's
own work starts. The scope probe (`HtmlPageObjectBase.cs:76-100`) reads the URL (local), then
`IsIdle` (`PlaywrightHtmlDriver.cs:552-563`: `WaitForLoadStateAsync(NetworkIdle, 10 ms)`, one trip,
and a `TimeoutException` thrown and caught on every attempt while the page is still loading), then
finds `<body>` (`CountAsync`, one trip) and reads `data-busy` (one trip). Then the element
(`CountAsync` plus a positional `Nth(0)`, `PlaywrightTestContext.cs:151-155`), then `IsVisibleAsync`
(`PlaywrightHtmlElement.cs:352`), then `ClickAsync` with the remaining budget, which auto-waits for
visible, stable, enabled and unobscured all over again. A container adds its parent's probe and
its own root lookup per attempt (`ContainerBase.cs:298-308`); a row in a collection in a container
is four scopes deep. Network idle also says nothing a Blazor Server page needs: the circuit's
state is `data-busy`, which the page already requires (`BlazorPageObjectBase.cs:329`).

The waits themselves are right. Polling is 100 ms, the default budget 5 s, page load 10 s
(`TimeoutSettings.cs:9-26`); nothing waits with Playwright's 30 s default (`OutsideACallMs` 5 s,
`PlaywrightHtmlElement.cs:302`); a read waits 250 ms for a node that is gone and then reports it
stale for the poll to find again (`:318-347`), so a re-render costs one interval, not the call.
`FindElements` hands out `Nth(i)` locators and the contract has rows re-find by key, so a
re-rendered list does not mislead a row object.

## Html: the browser, the app, the evidence

Tomorrow runs one app process per run (`TomorrowApp.cs:33`), eleven collections over it
(`xunit.runner.json` `maxParallelThreads` 10 plus `Serial`), and each collection fixture launches
its own Playwright, Chromium, context and page (`PlaywrightTestContext.cs:63-99`). A browser serves
every test and scenario of its collection; sign-out is `ClearCookiesAsync`
(`TomorrowAppFixture.cs:365`), which `ResetAppState` also is (`PlaywrightTestContext.cs:195-198`),
so `localStorage` carries from one scenario to the next: RW-140 is a Brinell gap, not a Tomorrow one.

No Html failure gets a screenshot. `IScreenshotService` has no Html implementation (none in
`Brinell.Html`, `.Blazor` or `.Playwright`); UAT looks for a `ScreenshotService` property on the fixture
(`UatScenarioResults.cs:115-127`) and Tomorrow's has none; the xUnit tier would need
`ScreenshotTestAttribute.CaptureIfFailed`, which nothing in Tomorrow calls. MAUI's fixture base
makes one for itself (`MauiTestFixtureBase.cs:137-149`). Every row in the register that "passes
alone" was diagnosed from text.

The register's flaky rows share one shape: a default budget on a loaded machine. RW-138 (People's
first load outlasts 5 s), RW-217 (three 35 s timeouts under load), RW-237 (three failing while another
agent's Android run shared the machine), RW-94 (once in a full run). The near-miss log would show
which calls run close (`ControlCall.cs:84-96`, half the budget or three replacements), but it is
off unless `BRINELL_CALL_LOG` is set and it writes a CSV per context, not a summary. Tomorrow's seven
sleeps (`HerePage.Board.cs:44, 188, 221, 488, 550, 721`; `DrawnCounts.cs:28`) all wait for an attribute
or a text to satisfy a predicate (a count above zero, a camera further east, a clock that changed);
Html controls offer `WaitAttribute(name, expected)` and `WaitText(expected)` for equality only
(`ControlBase.gen.cs:171`), so the page objects wrote their own loops.

## MAUI: Android and iOS

**Launch and attach.** `MauiTestFixtureBase` opens one Appium session in its constructor
(`MauiTestFixtureBase.cs:35-50`) through `MauiDriverFactory` by reflection over Appium.Net
(`MauiDriverFactory.cs:99-110`), with the APK as `app`, a reinstall unless
`BRINELL_APP_PREINSTALLED` (`MauiFixture.cs:525`), `appWaitPackage`, and `waitForIdleTimeout` 100 from
the fixtures over the factory's 500 (`MauiFixture.cs:521`, `ShellFixture.cs:158`, `MauiDriverFactory.cs:183`).
A session costs 20 s preinstalled and 32 to 43 s with the reinstall; the mobile head runs its two
collections one after the other (`Brinell.Maui.UITests.Mobile/AssemblyInfo.cs`). A lost session is
now replaced once per test (ee38eee1), which turned a crash from 200 lost tests into one.

**Finding and waiting.** Every lookup is one `POST /elements` at 174 ms mean on the emulator,
a tap 54 to 311 ms, `displayed` 33 to 57 ms (`.my/brinell/appium-performance.md`). A call's attempt
is the page root, the element, then the action; 1529fbe5 dropped the `displayed` re-read on
Android since a found element is on screen there (`AppiumMauiElement.cs:164`). Per test the
fixture pays `ReturnToHub` (one lookup, a back, the hub's `WaitLoaded`), the hub button's click and
`WaitForThePageToArrive`, a `Thread.Sleep(25)` loop whose every turn is a 174 ms lookup
(`MauiFixture.cs:143-192`), so the sleep is noise and the loop is bounded only by its 10 s. A
scroll-to-find is up to twelve swipes down and twelve up, a lookup after each, without looking at
the call's budget (`AppiumMauiDriver.cs:592-623`, `MaxScrollSwipes` 12): a miss costs about
twenty-four swipes and twenty-five lookups, near nine seconds inside one attempt. After a hit,
`WaitUntilPositionSettles` reads `Rect` every 50 ms until two reads agree (`AppiumMauiElement.cs:813-838`)
and `NudgeClearOfBottomEdge` scrolls again; `ScrollIntoViewAndroid` has a loop of its own with a
`DateTime.UtcNow` deadline and a `Pause(150)` (`:692-749`), and swallows every non-fatal error.

**What flakes on an emulator.** The UiAutomator2 server crashes at `waitForIdleTimeout` 0 and was
clean at 100 in one night's runs; nothing in the factory turns device animations off
(`disableWindowAnimation`), so a fresh emulator without the runbook's three `adb shell settings`
taps during transitions. "Found means visible" counts an element under a dialog as found, so the
tap lands on the dialog and the confirmation reports "accepted and did not take it": correct by R0,
and it reads as a flake. Two `Rect` reads 50 ms apart can agree while a fling still moves in bursts.
`ReturnToHub` presses back up to three more times while the hub is not showing
(`MauiFixture.cs:262-278`); on a slow emulator a hub that is late rather than covered gets a back
on the root activity, the app exits, the session is lost and the next test pays a restart.

**What iOS assumes, untested.** The routes in a7d989c7 were written from UIKit's documentation.
Five look wrong or costly on paper: `IsAtNavigationRoot` wants a button named `BackButton`
(`AppiumMauiDriver.cs:335-336`), where UIKit names the back button after the previous page's title
or "Back", so it will answer "at the root" on every page and every test pays the 10 s recovery
the note warns of; `IsFlyoutOpen` is "the flyout's table is in the tree" (`AppiumMauiElement.cs:1389-1399`),
and XCUITest keeps off-screen views in the tree with `visible="false"`, so it reads open always;
the factory bounds the idle wait on Android only (`MauiDriverFactory.cs:216-224`) while XCUITest
waits for quiescence before each command by default, the same 10 s trap on an animating page;
`TurnToIndex` steps a wheel back up to 500 rows, one round trip each (`Wheels.cs:71-90`);
and the fixture's defaults are `iPhone 15` and `17.0` (`MauiTestFixtureBase.cs:268-269`) against
the runbook's `iPhone 17 Pro Max` and 26.5. Plausible but to measure: wheels for date and time
need MAUI's pickers in wheel style, alert buttons with the cancel first, the hub leaving the tree on
a push, the edge swipe from x = 1 with a 0.1 s drag (`AppiumMauiDriver.cs:274-286`).
